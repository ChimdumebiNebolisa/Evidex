# Evidex editorial supplement

Companion to the final editorial revision. Sections S1–S5 contain existing documentation and supporting records. Results and statistical definitions are unchanged. The links below resolve within this supplementary package. This assembly performs no new scientific audit.

## S2 Complete statistical and outcome records

Included records: [OUTCOME_SUMMARY.csv](S2/OUTCOME_SUMMARY.csv), [COMPARISON_CASES.csv](S2/COMPARISON_CASES.csv), [IDENTICAL_PROMPT_VARIABILITY.csv](S2/IDENTICAL_PROMPT_VARIABILITY.csv), [BOOTSTRAP_REPRODUCED.csv](S2/BOOTSTRAP_REPRODUCED.csv), [STATISTICAL_AUDIT.json](S2/STATISTICAL_AUDIT.json), [MANUSCRIPT_RESULTS.json](S2/MANUSCRIPT_RESULTS.json).

The present experiment reuses the fixed 10,000-record FEVER development slice: 5,000 Supported and 5,000 Refuted records, with Not Enough Info excluded. The historical extraction records pseudorandom sampling seed 42; the fresh experiment did not draw a new sample. Each input retains its previously selected annotated evidence set, including exact sentence strings and joining format. Preparation checked 11,137 selected sentence pointers against the local Wikipedia archive. No alternative evidence set, label repair, or exclusion was chosen after observing the present experiment’s outcomes. ``Selected gold evidence'' therefore names the supplied benchmark-designated set, not retrieved evidence or a guarantee that the executed text is sufficient under every interpretation.

The models were requested as `gpt-5.4` and `gpt-5.4-mini`; all returned identifiers were respectively `gpt-5.4-2026-03-05` and `gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17`, with 90,000 responses each. Recorded provider creation times span October 17, 2026, from 00:31 to 13:41 UTC. Each model–claim–condition cell has three fresh calls, producing $10{,}000\times2\times3\times3=180{,}000$ responses. The joint schedule was shuffled with seed 20261016 across claims, models, conditions, and repetition indices; workers could finish in a different order. Every call used one user message with no system/developer message, prior history, retrieval, tools, or appended run identifier. Temperature was 0, reasoning effort `none`, completion cap 32 tokens, $n=1$, non-streaming, storage disabled, and requested service tier `default`. No API sampling seed varied between repetitions. Table 1 distinguishes the message changes; Appendix A gives the exact templates.

Repetition 1 and contrasts A-to-B and B-to-C were designated primary before the fresh outcomes; A-to-C is secondary. ``First pass'' denotes that prespecified index, not chronological request order in the shuffled schedule. All-three summaries include repetition 1 and are dependent repeated measures. Uncertainty uses the frozen 2,000-draw nonparametric bootstrap of 9,855 casefolded, whitespace-normalized claim-text clusters, seed 20261016. The 10,000 IDs include 271 records in 126 duplicate-text clusters. Each draw uses the same cluster weights across models, conditions, repetitions, and labels, retaining the original observed label-stratum proportions. A cluster with records in both label strata receives one shared weight. Repetitions are reduced within claim before this resampling. The intervals describe case-composition uncertainty conditional on the recorded collection period and model configuration; they are descriptive, not simultaneous hypothesis tests. No isolated post hoc significance claim or equivalence test is made. Independent reconstruction reproduced 58 full-set estimates and intervals plus 34 fresh-outcome estimates within the separately reported historically selected cohort.

OUTCOME_SUMMARY.csv partitions every model/contrast/repetition into the four groups. Each per-repetition table uses 10,000 assigned pairs; all-repetition rows use 30,000 pairs but only 10,000 claim IDs. A distinct-claim count in an outcome group is the union of IDs showing that outcome, so distinct counts across groups can overlap and must not be summed as independent claims. COMPARISON_CASES.csv contains all 60,000 model–claim–contrast vectors, with each vector's four outcome counts summing to three. IDENTICAL_PROMPT_VARIABILITY.csv contains 60,000 model–claim–condition cells, each retaining three unordered label pairs and six ordered directional pairs. These complete records remain the source for mixed patterns not compressed into the main tables.

Secondary A-to-C all-three recurrence is 208 model–claim cases across 174 IDs. Across any matched repetition it appears in 320 model–claim cases across 264 IDs. These combine evidence and instruction changes, rather than adding evidence alone. Fresh outcomes within the historically selected cohort are reported separately in the frozen summary; they are not the historical predictors' recorded responses.

The stale external-download qualification in the old manuscript is superseded only to the extent documented in S5. The executed sentence strings and benchmark labels remain unchanged.

Master seed 20261017 and documented derived group seeds select without replacement within each capped group. Deduplication retains every selection flag; claim-level features are reused across models and contrasts. The realized sample is 465 cases across 416 distinct claims. A 30-claim pilot spanning the outcome groups informed the codebook; definitions were fixed before coding the other 386 claims, and the pilot was recoded. Multiple feature labels are allowed. A separate assessment distinguishes supplied text that supports the reference, apparently conflicts with it, is insufficient, or permits competing readings. These annotations never change reference labels or correctness scores.

A subsequent unblinded AI recheck examined all 43 original apparent-conflict flags against complete frozen text, original local FEVER annotations, and both models' repeated predictions. A fixed-seed second-review sample, master seed 20261018, selected six non-conflict claims per outcome route and up to two per feature category, without replacement within group; deduplication yielded 56 claims. Numeric-bound, polarity, and wording-tag problems prompted a focused review of every original NUM, NEG, or TXT assignment: 190 claims, including already reviewed cases. In total, 230 claims received second review, 99 in full and 131 additionally for those rules; 186 claims did not receive second review. The same AI assistant performed both coding passes, with outcomes visible. Version 2 documents 50 changed claim annotations, including 29 assessment changes and 34 feature changes with overlap. This is corrective review, not independent coding, human validation, or an agreement study. The linked S3 records preserve selection, decisions, and coverage.

Of the 43 original conflict flags, the recheck classifies 34 as plausible text/reference conflicts, two as clear coding mistakes, three as needing additional context, and four as unresolved interpretations. The latter nine are revised to insufficient or ambiguous assessments. Across the full selected set, version 2 contains 193 supports-reference, 34 apparent-conflict, 96 insufficient, and 93 ambiguous claim-level assessments, denominator 416. These selected counts include 186 claims not second-reviewed and must not be presented as validated error prevalence. Spelling pairs also show why the same rule must apply across outcomes: Kojol/Kajol had previously received different identity-tolerance treatment in claims 38599 and 155934. Both are now marked ambiguous. Human review remains pending for every flag; no confirmed benchmark-error count or independent-coder agreement statistic is reported.

The original sampling has twelve model-specific groups. All-three A-to-B eligibility is 87 main and 99 mini cases; all-three B-to-C eligibility is 32 and 27. Main-model first-pass A-to-B eligibility is 857 rescues, 285 stayed-wrong cases, and 8,760 stayed-correct cases; mini's corresponding counts are 1,200, 310, and 8,345, with 30 selected from each group. Mixed-regression eligibility is 25 main and 90 mini cases, with 20 selected from each. Group seeds are the first eight SHA256 bytes, as a big-endian integer, of the master seed, model, and route joined by separators. Python Random/sample uses sorted string IDs without replacement within group. Case identity is claim ID, model, and contrast; annotation reuse is at claim ID. No A-to-C taxonomy sampling group was requested or added.

Version 1 used a 30-claim pilot, frozen definitions, pilot recoding, and complete reading of all selected claim/evidence/prediction records. Version 2 retains that history and clarifies application boundaries after the second review. NUM is a relevant precision or comparison demand, not a rule that every unequal number contradicts. NEG requires denial or reversed propositional polarity, not negative sentiment. TXT requires a material wording/spelling/extraction issue beyond the substantive semantic mismatch. A statement about one format, role, or place need not exclude every other one. The recheck applies identity tolerance and missing-warrant rules across outcome groups, with each decision recorded. Definitions and examples remain in REGRESSION_CODEBOOK_v2.md.

The other feature codes identify scope or degree (SCOPE), relations or semantic roles (REL), entity or attribute fillers (ENT), semantic readings or granularity (LEX), contextual linkage (CTX), and a missing warrant in the selected text (GAP). DIRECT is a fallback for an ordinary local match or mismatch with no distinctive feature; UNCLEAR retains unresolved coding. Codes can overlap, except that DIRECT cannot accompany a substantive feature. The separate reference-assessment axis and pending-review status prevent these features from becoming mutually exclusive error mechanisms.

REFERENCE_CONFLICT_REVIEW.html and its CSV give all 43 original flag IDs, complete selected evidence, original FEVER labels/pointers, both models' A/B/C predictions for all repetitions, original justifications, revised classifications, and reasons. CODING_CHANGE_LOG.csv records all changed annotations; ANNOTATION_REVIEW_COVERAGE.csv explicitly marks full, targeted, and absent second review for all 416 claims. TAXONOMY_CASES_v2.csv retains the original fields alongside revised coding for all 465 cases. Selection files and all original annotations remain unchanged. Human review is pending; the same-assistant recheck is not independent-coder agreement. Apparent-conflict counts are not confirmed reference errors, and none has been subtracted from the frozen benchmark denominator.
