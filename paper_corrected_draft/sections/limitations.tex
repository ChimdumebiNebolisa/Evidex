\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

All adjudication is automated silver labeling.
The Grok, GLM and Claude judgments are model outputs, not expert human annotation, and they create no new gold standard; no adjudication outcome was reviewed by humans.
The adjudication can therefore describe how model judges read the evidence, but not how people would read it.
Agreement within a panel also overstates how far independent human raters would agree, because the five judges are isolated runs of one locked model rather than independent readers.
Across panels, the category shares differ by judge family, so we report both complete panels rather than a single estimate.
Coverage is also uneven: the Claude panel judges only the 226 regressions, not the 1{,}060-claim judged cohort, and the GLM panel covers Stage~A only.

FEVER labels remain the reference standard for scoring GPT, and the experiment covers a narrow slice of FEVER.
Its claims are 2017-era Wikipedia claims restricted to the Supported and Refuted labels, and the evidence is designated rather than retrieved.
The results therefore say nothing directly about \texttt{NOT ENOUGH INFO} claims, about open-web retrieval, where retrieval errors would add to the failures studied here, or about claims from other sources and periods.
Both evaluated systems are GPT-5.4 variants, so the overlap between their transitions may partly reflect errors shared within one model family.
Disagreement between an NLI model or a judge panel and FEVER marks candidate ambiguity; it does not show that an annotation is wrong.

The design locates where a judge panel's verdict changes, not what happened inside GPT.
Progressive disclosure observes judges, not GPT-internal mechanisms, and the original GPT runs stored predicted labels only, so no analysis in this paper can recover why a particular prediction changed.
Each claim also has a single recorded prediction per model and condition, so the study does not measure run-to-run variability, and an individual transition may partly reflect it.

The adjudication itself was corrected after the fact.
The historical Stage~C packets displayed corrupted sentence text, and the historical taxonomy conflated disagreement with FEVER with evidence utilization.
The repaired Stage~C and the descriptive taxonomy were specified post hoc, after the historical results and defects had been inspected; the correction protocol is not a preregistration, and the taxonomy awaits coauthor review.
Stage~C also adds alternative evidence text for some claims, so changes associated with Stage~C are not a pure manipulation of representation.
Finally, each consensus path combines retained historical Stage~A and~B judgments with new Stage~C judgments made on a different occasion.
Treating those judgments as one path relies on the historical protocol's fresh context for every judge, batch and stage, which could not be verified independently because raw historical session transcripts were unavailable; if that isolation failed, some stage-to-stage changes could reflect earlier context rather than the newly disclosed evidence.
Historical execution events in the original Claude panel (a quarantined rerun after a blinding leak, a paused and resumed stage, and a duplicate-batch tie-break) affect only its retained Stage~A/B judgments and are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:historical-deviations}.

The Stage~C analysis also rests on an amendment adopted after the judgments and their provisional results had been seen.
The amendment relaxed the protocol's execution rules and selected the accepted outputs where earlier valid outputs existed, so under the original rules the protocol's primary analysis is incomplete and the amended analysis is not a pre-specified result.
The amendment governs which outputs are admitted, not how they are analysed, and the first-valid alternative changes six Claude consensus labels without changing any significance decision.
Execution provenance rests on operator records and hashed logs rather than provider attestation.
The protocol's follow-up panels (a newly selected residual Claude panel, a Stage~C resolver, and an optional fixed historical residual panel) were not run.
